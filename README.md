# CS330
Computational Graphics and Visualization

When I start designing software, I focus on the purpose behind what I am creating and the people who will use it. I like to think through how every part connects before I write a single line of code. This project taught me that planning and structure are just as important as creativity. Breaking down a big idea into smaller, manageable parts helped me stay organized and focused while keeping the design simple and effective.

Working on this project gave me new skills in visual and functional design. I learned how geometry, lighting, and color can all work together to make a 3D environment feel realistic. I became more aware of how details like shadows, reflections, and material textures impact the final result. This helped me understand that good design in software is not just about how something looks, but also about how everything functions together as a system.

Throughout the process, I followed a clear design path that moved from simple to complex. I started by creating basic objects, then refined them by adding lighting, texture, and camera controls. Each milestone built on the last, and that step by step method made it easier to identify issues and improve the project over time. This approach gave me confidence in tackling bigger tasks while maintaining quality in every stage.

These design habits are something I can carry into future work. The idea of starting small and improving through testing and feedback can be applied to any software project. I also plan to continue structuring my code and visuals before diving into development, since that organization made everything smoother and easier to manage.

In terms of development, I’ve learned to approach coding with a problem solving mindset. I take time to understand what I’m trying to achieve, write smaller parts of the program, and test frequently. Working this way prevents major issues later and makes debugging easier. I’ve learned that neat, well commented code is not just a school requirement but it’s a skill that saves time and helps me think more clearly as a developer.

One of the biggest new strategies I picked up was modular programming. Dividing code into smaller, reusable sections made it easier to maintain and test. I also developed better control over lighting and shader adjustments, learning how precision in small changes can completely alter how the 3D scene looks and feels.

Iteration played a huge role in my development process. I constantly tested the scene, adjusted textures, lighting angles, and object placement, and then tested again. This cycle helped me understand how every decision in code impacts what appears on screen. By the end of the project, I was more patient and deliberate about improving one element at a time instead of trying to fix everything at once.

Over the course of all the milestones, my approach to programming evolved a lot. At first, I focused mainly on getting things to work. As I went on, I started thinking more about clarity, organization, and optimization. I learned to write cleaner code, apply better structure, and manage the interaction between the camera, lighting, and textures more effectively. It felt like moving from just coding something to actually engineering a finished product.

Computer science has become a key part of how I see my future. It’s helping me think more logically and creatively at the same time. I want to build tools and applications that make an impact, and understanding how to combine design and programming is the foundation for that.

This project also showed me how computational graphics connects math, physics, and programming in ways I hadn’t seen before. It gave me new skills that will help me in future classes, especially in areas like game design or data visualization. I now understand the value of blending technical skill with visual creativity.

In a professional sense, these new abilities in computational graphics and visualization give me a real advantage. I’ve learned how to bring concepts to life through design and programming, which is valuable in fields like simulation, software development, and even animation. It taught me that computer science is not just about writing lines of code but it’s about creating experiences that can help people see and interact with ideas in completely new ways.
